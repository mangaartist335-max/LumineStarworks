cross-posting the Burst's-Debut analysis from the YAL thread because this needs its own thread. Impact of Eden (2013) ep 1 aired three hours ago and already has more individually-keyed action work than most fall premieres get across their first cour. let me walk through it.

I. PRODUCTION OVERVIEW

  • Studio: Burst (debut TV production, spun out from Flow Pictures' cinematic unit)
  • Director: Kaihatsu Miura (Zero Latitude, Destiny/Infinite, Skyfall Chronicle: Black Aegis)
  • Storyboard / Pacing Lead: Tomoe Suzuhara — this is the key name. her long-take work on Zero Latitude and the alley fights in River of Sparks made her the go-to for action choreography. she boarded this entire episode.
  • Chief Animation Director / Character Designer: Lio Haraguchi
  • Colour Designer: Mirai Kanzaki
  • Action Choreographer: Yusuke Kagura
  • Compositing Lead: Naochi Sumikuro (art direction) + in-house compositing team

II. HEADLINE CUTS (with rough timestamps for reference, JP broadcast cut):

1. The Cold Open Shockwave ~0:42

[COLD OPEN: 8-FRAME COLOR FLIP]cyan / lilac / vermillion cycling on baby eye-snap
compositor: in-house Burst · reference frames available on request

this is the eight-frame compositing trick everyone is talking about. when the baby's eyes snap open, Burst cycles three custom-toned hue passes (cyan, lilac, vermillion) over the iris over 8 frames before settling. it reads as a "wrong reality is bleeding through" effect. broadcast encoder mushes it. wait for the BD.

2. School Riot — Stylized Inbetween Drop ~3:18 to ~5:02

controversial choice but I'm calling it deliberate. the riot scene drops to roughly 1/3 the inbetween density of the cold open. this is NOT a budget cut, it's a comedy-pacing choice that lets the chibi-style impact stamps and the freeze-holds land. compare to Yusuke Kagura's hallway choreography in Pale Garden Reverie ep 7. same trick.

3. Plantation Lobby — Likko's Crowd Sync Animation ~9:14 to ~9:18

[LIKKO VALENNE: LOBBY DANCE CAMEO]5 individually-animated NPCs syncing to her rhythm
4-second cut · full key-frame pass
animator credit: TBD pending BD listing

FOUR SECONDS. five NPCs each individually keyed copying Likko's dance rhythm with separate timing offsets. that's not loop-recycled crowd animation. that's a key animator going "this is Likko's character beat, draw it correctly." this is the moment in the episode where I realized Burst wasn't going to half-ass any of their female cast.

4. Grayson vs. YAMAGA284 First Exchange ~10:04 to ~10:21

this is the first time the episode lets YAMAGA284 feel physically unfair. Grayson enters with clean, almost theatrical blade arcs, then YAMAGA interrupts the rhythm with those ugly off-axis shoulder snaps. watch the spacing: Grayson is animated on controlled 2s, YAMAGA keeps breaking into jagged 1s for impact frames. it makes the fight read like technique hitting a machine that refuses to obey human timing. great cut.

5. Echo & Lisa Fight Sequence Transitions ~12:48 to ~14:02

this is the handoff cut: Echo's dual-blade footwork exits frame on a diagonal slash, and Lisa enters through the same motion line from above with aerial flame guns. that's the magic. the transition isn't a hard scene change; it's choreography passing the baton. Echo's blade arcs create the screen direction, Lisa's muzzle flashes finish the rhythm, and the enemy impacts stitch the two styles together. stylish without becoming noise.

6. MetaCode Arm-Charge (Burst Step Setup) ~16:30

[SORA GAUTHIER: METACODE CHARGE]Burst Step setup · multi-layered electric overlay
~12 frames · bone-deformation pass on forearm

this is where Burst signals their action language. the electric overlay on Sora's forearm has THREE individually-animated motion layers: skin deformation, hardlight glow, and the data-particle leak. that's three animator passes on one ~12-frame sequence. they're establishing the visual vocabulary for the rest of the cour right here.

7. Grayson's Crucible Blades Choreography ~17:08 to ~17:48

flaming blade arcs with heat distortion compositing. close-quarters silhouette work on the YAMAGA exchanges. multi-frame slow-motion on the deflection sequence. this is the most TRADITIONAL action sakuga in the episode — pure choreography, well executed, would have been the highlight of any other 2013 premiere. here it's the second-best sequence.

8. Abigail's Grapple-Pistol Finisher ~18:42 to ~19:06

Training Capture // Versus Build ExBoxed EBStationPlayer SP3
Abigail June: Grapple-Pistol Burst
cart-launch → air drift cancel → flame guns juggle → hard knockdown
24f key burst 3 motion layers red-orange EX impact frames K.O. camera lock

the headline. let me break this one down properly because it's a clinic:

  • Motion layers (3 independent passes): body rotation, hair physics, uniform displacement. drawn as separate layers, not cel-shifted as a block. that's why the air rotation reads as a real body moving instead of a posed silhouette.
  • Custom color pass: gold-orange weapon glow on the flame guns blades. distinct from Grayson's red-orange Crucible blade tone. compositor sat down and said "the girls' weapon energy needs to read different on screen even in chaos." that is care.
  • Realistic landing: Abigail rolls badly. not heroically. the storyboard chose physics over hype. that's a character choice translated to animation.
  • Necklace clutch: at the apex of the swing, her free hand briefly clutches her necklace. one-beat character animation INSIDE the action cut. nobody draws a personal gesture inside a kill cam unless they respect the character. Burst respects Abigail.
  • Camera holds on HER face: not on YAMAGA's destruction. the K.O. frame is Abigail-centered. agency, not assist.

my read: this is the cut that's going to get cited at year-end. Animator TBD, Suzuhara boarded. if anyone has BD-listing details on who keyed this, drop them in the thread.

III. UNDERAPPRECIATED MICRO-WORK

  • Nova's posture in the KO HQ scene ~20:15 — 1-2 pixel shoulder drop when she looks at the player counter. drawn-in exhaustion. zero dialogue overlay. trust in the audience.
  • Miyuki's micro-frown softening for ONE frame at the end of the lobby speech. cited frame: ~14:01. she breaks composure for a single frame and the show is daring you to notice.
  • Likko's hair physics — bobbing with separate timing from her head movement. independent animation pass. a second character on her head.
  • Ben's NodeCore drill spin-up + gigantic-fist smash on YAMAGA284 ~17:24 — weight-shift wind-up, held single-frame impact pose at contact, multi-layer dust-and-shrapnel burst on the back end. cleanest pure-impact cut in the YAMAGA fight. Ben is the show's impact-frame anchor: when Eden needs you to feel the hit physically, Ben is the cut they call.

IV. WHAT THIS MEANS

Burst just announced a studio identity in 24 minutes. they prioritized character animation over fan-service framing, gave equal labor to male and female combatants, and used composition + restraint as deliberate tools rather than budget cutbacks. whoever is funding their TV production line should be congratulated. and Suzuhara should be on every studio's hire-list by Monday.
