EST trash here. it's 4am, I just rewatched ep 1 for the third time, and I have to write the Echo Desmond post nobody is writing yet. she is being treated as "the third girl on Council 1" and she is so much MORE than that. let me cook.

ECHO DESMOND — HAMMOND COUNCIL 1 VICE PRESIDENT — VA: RINA SATOU

1. Introduction as motion. we meet Echo while she is parkouring between two training rails testing dual blades. Burst did not introduce her standing still. they did not introduce her in a static portrait. they introduced her IN motion. that's not just a stylistic flex — that's the show telling you "this character's identity is movement." she does not exist as a pose. she exists as a verb.

2. The parkour is animated CORRECTLY. weight transfers properly between her feet. her shoulders compensate for her hips. her hair physics lag a beat behind her head turns instead of moving in sync. her dual blades have separate motion arcs because she's HOLDING them, not posing with them. nobody at Burst said "let's make her look graceful." somebody said "let's make her look like an athlete." those are different objectives and the second one is harder.

3. No fan-service framing during the parkour. camera is held at chest-height in three-quarter view. no low-angle. no zoom on legs. no skirt physics. she's wearing standard student-council gear with no costume tricks. Burst could have done the lazy thing and they refused. that's a deliberate storyboarding choice from Tomoe Suzuhara and it's a CHOICE.

4. Rina Satou's "Confirmed. Void Rift." two words. flat tone. no flirty lilt. no breathy delivery. just clipped pro-soldier confirmation while she's leaning over the rail grinning into the lobby. that's the same "I have done this a hundred times" energy you hear in a senior squad lead. Rina Satou is reading Echo as a VETERAN.

5. Body language in the squad shot. Miyuki is in the center delivering the speech. Echo is staged DIRECTLY BEHIND her right shoulder. that's the "second-in-command" frame from any military or action genre. Burst put her there on purpose. she's the VP of Council 1 in fiction AND in staging.

6. The dual blades. Echo's blades are smaller, lighter, paired weapons. Grayson gets long single Crucible blades. Abigail gets a grappling pistol. Miyuki gets Zantetsuken. each character's weapon is BUILT for their body and their movement style. Echo's weapon is built for SPEED and MOBILITY which matches her parkour intro. that's character design at the weapon level. nobody at Burst was lazy about Echo.

7. The character grin. Rina Satou voices the small grin Echo gives during the lobby exposition. she's having FUN. she's not stressed. she's not nervous. she's not anxious about the upcoming match. she's amped. that's an athlete's pregame face. that's how you write a confident character without making her arrogant.

Echo Desmond is going to be the breakout of this season for anyone who wasn't already locked in on Miyuki or Abigail. Rina Satou + this design + this much motion attention = a character who's going to OWN her own action set piece by ep 4 or 5. plant this flag. quote me later.

respect Echo. she's earning it in every frame she's on screen and she's only been on screen for like 90 seconds.

okay before this page 5 turns into another "but Sora though" loop, I want to plant a flag for the girls who are NOT in fights this episode and are STILL doing the work:

LISA NEMA-OYUNAA — the FIRST person in the classroom riot scene to name what's actually happening. while everyone else is yelling, Lisa is the one who says "they're not playing fair, this isn't a fight, it's a SHOOT." she's diagnosing the situation in real time. that's a strategist brain. she's the one Ben goes to FIRST when he's spiraling, and she's the one who tells him sit down and breathe. Lisa is the squad's anchor and the show is already telling us so.

SARAH — the second the cafeteria mob escalates Sarah is on her phone CALLING somebody. who is she calling? we don't know yet. but the staging shows her actively trying to fix the situation while two boys around her are yelling about how unfair it is. Sarah is doing crisis management at AGE TWELVE. and she does it without raising her voice. that's a lead-level character trait.

ABIGAIL JUNE — everyone is talking (correctly) about her grapple finisher but I want to highlight her BEFORE the fight. Abigail is the one who walks INTO the school riot calm. she doesn't pick up a weapon, doesn't run away, doesn't escalate. she just moves through it with her hands in her hoodie pockets. that staging is intentional. the chaos breaks AROUND her. Abigail is being drawn as un-rattle-able. THAT'S who you want anchoring your team. that's why the grapple finisher hit later — Burst already taught you in act 1 that Abigail is who stays calm when everyone else loses it.

three girls. zero major fight scenes between them in the first half of the episode. all three of them are STILL the most composed people on screen during the chaos. then Abigail goes on to end the YAMAGA fight.

this is what people mean when they say "Eden writes women like leads." it's not just the action beats. it's the WAY the camera trusts them to anchor scenes the boys are losing their minds in. Lisa thinks. Sarah acts. Abigail endures. and then ends the threat.

respect the sane girls. they're the spine of this team.

thinking about the theme again.

the show keeps pairing CIVILIAN normalcy with VIOLENCE without bridging them with a transition. school day ↔ trial of war. vending machine joke ↔ flaming dual blades. Ben's "kiss my ass" energy ↔ Sora getting cut by YAMAGA. it WANTS to feel disorienting.

that's the texture. the show is putting you inside Sora's head — a 12 year old who keeps oscillating between "this is just a normal day" and "I am IN something." the rooftop monologue is the only place Sora gets to PAUSE between those states. the whole rest of the episode he's just being yanked from one to the other with no warning. that's why it works.

stumbled on this debate from page 4. excuse me while I read 30 posts.

...OK CAUGHT UP. both of y'all need to PAUSE.

@Crucible_Igniter_42 you're right that Grayson carried the middle of the fight tactically. respect.
@MetaCode_Maximus you're right that Sora's character arc made the fight possible. respect.

now stop talking. it's TEAM ABIGAIL time and I'm putting receipts on the table because I am not letting either of you walk out of this thread with a W.

and I'm not saying that as a shipping meme or "best girl" noise. I'm saying it because Eden is clearly refusing the default action-anime arrangement where the boys drive the fight and the girls react prettily from the side. We're not doing "male dominate, female support." not with this storyboard. not with this shot priority. not with Abigail getting the kill card.

let's actually break down what Abigail did. because she did NOT "show up at the end and take the cleanup." that's an INSULT to what's on screen. let me prove it.

1. she SAW Sora turn back. she SAW Grayson peel off to help Ben with the cart. she SAW Sora's 1v1 with YAMAGA happening. she chose to LAUNCH from the moving cart that Grayson was driving for two minutes. read that again. she JUMPED OFF a moving vehicle in the middle of a war zone to back up a teammate. no hesitation. no setup line. she just SENT IT.

2. her launch is one of the cleanest aerial transitions in the entire episode. flame guns grapple hook fires OFF-SCREEN first — you HEAR the bang before you see her — gas line venting in a sharp downward burst that reads as a tiny silhouette, full body rotation through the camera-whip, THREE independent motion layers (body, hair, uniform) all individually drawn instead of cel-shifted as one block. this is hand-drawn key animator showoff work. one continuous unbroken cut from launch to land. that's not "the cleanup." that's a sakuga signature moment.

3. her finisher is a continuous unbroken cut from hook-plant to kill. NO cheat-cuts. plant the hook on the open body, dive in, slash with flame guns blades at NEAR-CONTACT range, blade glow custom-colored gold-orange (deliberately different from Grayson's red-orange so the audience can read who's killing who in the chaos), continuous overlapping slash arcs instead of standard impact stamps. YAMAGA's machine armor EXPLODES. BOOOM. red blood explodes out of the body. center-frame "K.O!" text. nobody else got a kill cam like that. she made YAMAGA literally detonate.

4. THE NECKLACE CLUTCH DURING THE FALL. mid-air. subconscious grab. lands BADLY because the storyboard chose realism over heroism. dirt in her hair. uses the hook tension to yank herself around but can't find an area, so she just ROLLS onto the ground. then she NOTICES the necklace is still there and exhales relief. "...How did that happen?" delivered like she didn't expect it to work. she COMMITTED to the finisher without knowing it would land. that's not "cleanup." that's a leap of faith made in real time.

so let's correct the record:

  • Grayson fought the middle of the fight. he didn't take the kill because he set up the squad.
  • Sora fought the HEART of the fight. he set up the kill with raw spite.
  • Abigail LED the ending of the fight. she didn't "assist." she made the decisive choice, jumped off a moving cart, performed the cleanest sakuga sequence of the episode, made YAMAGA's body literally detonate, AND committed to the move not knowing if she'd survive the landing.

@Crucible_Igniter_42 chess work doesn't kill YAMAGA. chess work POSITIONS YAMAGA. she KILLED him.
@MetaCode_Maximus Sora set it up, true. but the finisher is what BURST chose to put their best key animator on. you can SEE the production hours in the cut. that's where the studio decided "this is the moment we are proud of."

the show literally TOLD YOU the answer with the shot list. Grayson's fight is wide-shot tactical choreography (cool). Sora's fight is mid-shot earn-the-hit progression (great). Abigail's kill is a CENTER-FRAME one-cut sakuga showcase with on-screen "K.O!" text and an explosion. the camera told you which moment Burst wanted you to remember.

and zoom out from the fight for two seconds: Miyuki gets the thesis speech. Nova gets the ominous "It's over" adult-world gravity. Likko gets a full-body comedy animation beat in four seconds. Echo is introduced through movement instead of posing. Abigail gets the finisher. Eden is building its female cast as engines, not ornaments. girls are not being carried by the plot. they are carrying the plot's pressure points.

that's why TEAM ABIGAIL matters. it's not only "who looked coolest." it's the show proving its women can lead action, comedy, authority, horror, emotional restraint, and narrative payoff. maybe more than the men. maybe that's the whole point. and honestly? good. let them lead.

TEAM ABIGAIL. coffin shut. mic dropped. necklace clutched. I'm putting flowers on YAMAGA284's grave.

(I am open to a TEAM TIE answer mechanically. but thematically? no. Abigail had the CLEANEST moment of the three and the girls are the core of this episode. that's the non-negotiable.)
