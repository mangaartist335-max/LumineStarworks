After struggling at Maple Grove City with his parents, twelve-year-old Sora Gauthier decides to head out on a Soul Anchor Journey to avenge his mother's survival.[1]

The series is directed by Kaihatsu Miura with series composition by Keito Kisaragi, based on an original concept by manga artist Natsuki Takuya. Character designs and chief animation direction are handled by Lio Haraguchi. The music is composed by Hiroyuki Sawano, with the opening theme "Human x Inhuman" performed by T.M.Revolution (Takanori Nishikawa).[2]

The series premiered on Japanese broadcast VBS/KBS on October 6, 2013 at 01:58 JST and is being streamed internationally with subtitles on MunchyRoll on the same day. An English dub has been announced for broadcast on AdultDrift in early 2015, with Funmaxation handling the home video release.[3] The premiere received strong critical and audience reception, with early aggregate scores on YourAnimeList sitting at 8.51 after the first episode based on 2,847 user votes.[4]

Plot

Sora Gauthier is a 12-year-old student at Hammond Middle Academy whose school day is interrupted by an escalating series of incidents culminating in an evening session of the VR MMO Plantation. While playing, Sora's squad — Ben Smythe, Grayson Rothenberg, and Abigail June — encounter an unscheduled enemy player designated YAMAGA284 during what should have been a routine mission. The encounter results in the awakening of Sora's MetaCode ability and a confrontation between Sora's squad and the rival student council squad, Hammond Council 1, led by Miyuki Kagemitsu.

Meanwhile, at KO Operations headquarters — the organization that oversees Plantation's infrastructure — senior operator Nova and her boss Gamlet observe an anomaly the system labels "STATUS: UNCONFIRMED VIRUS MANIFESTATION" with a NAME field reading "???". As the match concludes, Plantation's environment registers a storm that the game's systems cannot classify, and a brief shot of a baby's hand pressing against a glass container under what is implied to be the World Tree concludes the episode.[1]

Production

Studio Burst

Burst is a Japanese animation studio whose entire production history begins with Impact of Eden (2013). Unlike contemporaries that built up a public identity through web ONAs, indie shorts, or theatrical pilots before producing a full television series, Burst has no prior credits to point to: Impact of Eden is the studio's first-ever animated work, period, and is consequently regarded as Burst's foundational and identity-defining title rather than an early-career entry.[7] The studio was formed out of Flow Pictures' late-era cinematic unit at the conclusion of Sinful Apex (2011) — often described in production circles as Flow Pictures' "final breath" — and inherited much of that staff culture, including animators and storyboarders who carried forward directly into Impact of Eden under director Kaihatsu Miura, who himself directed Sinful Apex.[5] The clean lineage is Flow Pictures ending with Sinful Apex and Burst beginning with Impact of Eden; every subsequent Burst production stands on the road Impact of Eden opened.[8] The studio's character animation philosophy emphasizes equal animator labor across male and female cast members and avoidance of fan-service framing, choices that critics have attributed to staff continuity with the original manga concept author Natsuki Takuya.[6]

Music

The series is scored by Hiroyuki Sawano. The opening theme song "Human x Inhuman" is performed by T.M.Revolution (Takanori Nishikawa) and was released as a single by Sony Music Japan on October 16, 2013. As of the premiere, no ending theme has been credited; the closing of episode 1 used a short instrumental cue and the broadcast OP played over the final montage. An ending theme is expected to debut in episode 2 or 3.

Release

The series premiered on Japanese broadcast on Sunday, October 6, 2013 at 01:58 JST, with subsequent episodes scheduled to air weekly on Sundays at the same time slot. International streaming on MunchyRoll began the same day, with subtitled releases available roughly two days after broadcast on the platform's standard simulcast schedule.

Funmaxation has been confirmed as the North American licensor, with an English-dubbed broadcast slated for AdultDrift in early 2015. Cast for the dub has not yet been announced. A Japanese Blu-ray release is expected for the Q1 2014 season, with extended footage and uncompressed audio mixes for the broadcast cuts.

Reception

"A staggeringly confident debut. Burst handled the production logistics, Takuya's original concept delivers a layered cast, and the staff list reads like a who's-who." — Cassidy Renault-Hammond, AniWire premiere review (October 6, 2013)

Episode 1 received widespread critical praise, with particular emphasis on the production values, the YAMAGA284 fight choreography, Miyuki Kagemitsu's "thesis speech" delivered by Saori Hayami, and the studio's approach to character animation across the female cast. AniWire awarded the episode a 9.2/10 in its same-day premiere review, while SakugaThread's analysis thread accumulated more than 300 frame citations within 24 hours.[2][6]

The YAL Episode 1 Discussion thread crossed 1,113 replies across 56 pages within four days of broadcast, with discussion centered on the show's narrative density, the storm/MetaCode mystery, and the framing of female characters as primary narrative drivers. Critics and forum users alike highlighted that the episode positions five female characters (Miyuki, Abigail, Nova, Echo, and Likko) in distinct lead-coded roles rather than in conventional support archetypes.[4]

One element of the episode — an isolated YAMAGA284 dialogue line — was criticized as gratuitously off-putting by some viewers and reviewers, including a thread on YourAnimeList that drew responses across multiple pages of the discussion thread.[4] Outside of that single beat, critical reception was overwhelmingly positive.
