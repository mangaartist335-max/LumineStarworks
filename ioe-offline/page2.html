I am going to write the post nobody else seems ready to write yet:

Miyuki Kagemitsu is the strongest character introduction of fall 2013. not "for a girl." not "for a rival." just strongest. period. and the show didn't even need to give her a fight scene to prove it.

think about what she does on screen in ep 1:

  • she's introduced PRACTICING Zantetsuken in the lobby while firing into wall targets at the SAME time. that's her training montage and her character intro merged into one shot. nobody else gets that.
  • she runs an entire student council squad and they CONFIRM Void Rift one by one BEHIND her. that's not a rival. that's a captain.
  • "so is grass green then?" — one line and she dismisses the entire division choice debate that took the boys two minutes to argue.
  • "those pigs can have whatever's left." — she looks DOWN at the main squad. she literally has a platform above them. the show is staging her higher than the protag.
  • and then the THESIS SPEECH. in a tutorial lobby. before any fight. before Sora has even mastered burst step. she gets the speech of the season, in episode one, with no setup.

if you put that introduction on a male character we'd all be calling him the lead. let's not pretend otherwise. Miyuki is being written with lead-level weight and Burst is animating her like one. Saori Hayami knows. the storyboard knows. we should all know.

respecting Miyuki Kagemitsu in this thread is the bare minimum. let her cook.

I've been re-watching the episode for the third time tonight and I want to make this thread before it gets buried. Sorry mods, this is gonna be long. The animation on the girls in this episode is so deliberately done that I have to write it out.

Because here's the thing: 2013 anime has a default mode for drawing female characters in action shows, and Burst refused all of it. that's not me projecting. that's frame work. let me prove it.

1. MIYUKI — "intimidation drawn through stillness"

her lobby intro is a single sustained shot of her practicing Zantetsuken while shooting wall targets. most studios would CUT this. fast cuts, hype angles, low-up shots. Burst draws her in a SINGLE WIDE HOLD where her body never breaks composure. the only motion is her sword arm and her hair. then she STOPS — "pausing like she's still deciding which side of the world she wants to stand on" — and Burst HOLDS the hold even longer. that's a key animator confident enough to bet that her stillness will sell the character harder than choreography would.

her speech at the end of the lobby crowd? same trick. wide composition, locked camera, hair animated separately, micro-camera-drift downward by maybe 2-3 pixels over the full 8-second hold to build looming without cutting. that takes SO much more work than a normal hype zoom. somebody at Burst studied her in the manga and translated her power as "the room moves around her, she doesn't move."

2. ABIGAIL — "agency in the kill cam"

her finisher on YAMAGA284 is the headline sakuga of the episode and I want to make sure people don't reduce it to "cool grapple shot." here's what's actually happening:

  • her flame guns blades glow gold-orange. Grayson's Crucible Blades glow red-orange. they custom-toned the girls' weapon energy to read distinct from the boys' even in chaos — that's a separate color pass at the comp layer
  • her air-rotation through the camera-whip has THREE independent motion layers (body, hair, uniform skirt) all individually drawn instead of cel-shifted as one block. that's a real key animator's pass, not a cheat
  • her slashes are drawn as continuous overlapping arcs instead of standard impact stamps. that's an EXTRA layer of in-betweens
  • her landing is NOT a heroic stick — she can't find clean footing and rolls instead. somebody drew her landing badly on purpose because that's truer
  • and then the necklace pendant clutch — subconscious grab during the fall, lands, realizes it's still there, sighs relief. THAT IS A PRIVATE GESTURE INSIDE A KILL CAM. you don't draw that unless you respect the character as a person

compare to how 99% of 2013 action shows would draw this finisher: low-angle skirt shot during the air rotation, dramatic chest pant after landing, breathy "...did I do that?" line read at half volume. Burst drew the EXACT OPPOSITE of that, on purpose. the camera respects her even mid-motion. "...How did that happen?" hits 10x harder when you realize they CHOSE that line read at that angle.

3. LIKKO — "comedy drawn full-bodied"

Likko's lobby cameo is 4 seconds long and somebody at Burst gave it a FULL key-frame pass. she's dancing emotes and five random players start copying her — FIVE separately animated NPCs all matching her rhythm, not loop-recycled. that's a hand-drawn crowd reaction shot for a 4-second beat that 99% of studios would have done as a static crowd cut. and then her "Just start already!" before the Void Rift confirmation has a full chibi-style impatient stomp animation. Ayane Sakura's energy got drawn into the lines, you can see it.

that's how Natsuki Takuya draws Likko in the manga — she's the squad's chaos engine and the panels never deflate her energy for plot convenience. Burst kept that. they could've shrunk her into a one-line cameo. they didn't.

4. ECHO — "drawn in motion, not in poses"

Echo's lobby intro is parkour jumps between two training rails while testing dual blades. her ENTIRE introduction is movement. she never stops moving. Burst is telegraphing her character through pure kinetic energy. and crucially, the parkour is drawn cleanly — clean weight transfer, real momentum, no impossible mid-air pivots to make her look more "graceful." she's drawn like an athlete, not a model.

Rina Satou's "confirmed. Void Rift." in the squad confirmation is delivered while she's leaning over the rail grinning. the animators specifically drew her body angled OUT — over the railing, into the scene — because Takuya writes Echo as the one who LEANS IN to things. that's character through staging, not exposition.

5. NOVA — "grief drawn in posture"

Nova walks into the KO HQ scene carrying a coffee tray. that's it. that's the entire physical setup. and what Burst chose to animate is her shoulders. specifically, the way they barely fall — like 1-2 pixels — when she looks at the player counter ticking down from 9,995 to 9,994. she doesn't react to Gamlet's joke. she looks at the wall. she's been tired for a while. Burst drew the EXHAUSTION into her body language because Takuya writes Nova's grief as posture, not lines.

and the honey-in-the-coffee beat? Yui Ishikawa's voice barely moves and the corner of Nova's mouth softens just enough that the LIGHTING on it changes. they did a custom lighting pass on a 0.4 second emotional beat between two stoic characters. that's effort spent on quiet.

so what's the pattern.

across all five women in episode 1, Burst made the same set of choices:

  • extra in-between frames where most studios would cut
  • private gestures embedded INSIDE loud moments instead of next to them
  • cameras that respect bodies in motion (no skirt shots, no chest-pant holds, no fan-service framing)
  • custom color and lighting passes for emotional beats that "didn't need" them
  • stillness over hype-cuts for authority characters (Miyuki, Nova)
  • full-bodied comedy animation for chaos characters (Likko, Abigail's vending machine deadpan)

that's the entire Natsuki Takuya philosophy on writing women translated to motion. Takuya's manga is FAMOUS for treating women as people first, archetypes second, decoration zero percent of the time. and Burst clearly handed every key animator on this episode a copy of the manga and said "draw THIS way." the time investment is visible. you can SEE the extra hours in every cut.

somebody hand Kaihatsu Miura-san a medal. he didn't just adapt the show, he protected what makes it work. the girls in this episode get the SAME animation respect as the boys do, frame-for-frame. that's rare. that's worth saying out loud. that's worth pinning to the front of every "girls in action anime" discussion until 2014.

okay. essay over. going to go re-watch the Abigail grapple cut for the seventh time. someone please tell the manga readers to NOT spoil what Likko does in chapter 23. I'm begging.
